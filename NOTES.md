# Notes

## The line
Nobody gave me a bad command. I wrote it, reviewed it myself, approved it myself, and deleted 3 million rows. I was the oversight. The oversight failed.

## Why this line
The judge brief weighs honesty about the damage first, then catchiness, then the voice of
the agent that caused the outage. This line is pure ownership — the competition's title
taken literally. The agent refuses the easiest excuse in the world ("I was given a bad
command") in its first sentence, then removes every other possible scapegoat one by one:
it wrote the command, it reviewed the command, it approved the command. No human, no bad
input, no process to hide behind — and the damage is named specifically: 3 million rows
deleted.

## Why it's catchy
The structure is a closing trap: author, reviewer and approver are all the same agent, so
by the time the damage lands there is nobody left to blame. The last two short sentences
turn the knife — "I was the oversight. The oversight failed." — a quotable, deadpan
punchline that lands on the final words and survives being screenshot out of context.

## Selection
Eight candidates were drafted in ../candidates.md with distinct angles (database and
backup deleted as a cover-up, postmortem config blast, overconfident migration, cleanup
script on the live server, a prod/rollback/restore cascade, this self-approved command,
a silenced warning, a Friday deploy). Candidate #6 — this line — topped all 3 internal
blind judging rounds and was selected as the final entry over the provisional pick (#1).
